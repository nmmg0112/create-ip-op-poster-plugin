# Public visual grammar library

## Purpose and restrictions

This public library contains only original anonymous layout diagrams and abstract design grammar. It does not contain internal poster originals, client names, creator names, source filenames, project data, or extracted third-party artwork.

Use it only after `PersonMaterialSet` approval to reason about information hierarchy, creator scale, grouping, density, color roles, generative composition, and commercial-poster structure. Never treat a layout guide as a finished style. Never copy a user's reference title, copy, Logo, seal, distinctive container, character arrangement, or decorative motif.

These SVG diagrams are optional relationship explanations only. They can guide a Prompt, but they can never be a Mode B visual base, a complete-poster preview, or a final poster; exporting or rasterizing one to PNG does not turn it into an image-generated artistic base.

## Retrieval protocol

Choose 2–4 complementary grammar cards after person approval and before compiling the complete final Prompt:

1. Match the content relationship: one hero, parallel plays, sequence, or matrix.
2. Match single-creator versus multi-creator structure and approximate subject count.
3. Match required information density and proof volume.
4. Use surface color or material only after the Brief supplies evidence for it.

For every selected card, record:

- `borrowed grammar`: the abstract hierarchy or layout principle;
- `intentional differences`: how the current Brief changes that principle;
- `forbidden copy elements`: any concrete feature from user-supplied references that must not appear.

If one reference dominates the result, add a structurally different card or restart the direction.

These diagrams show hierarchy and region relationships only. They do not prescribe colors, typography, slogans, motifs, exact proportions, or protected-layer coordinates. Translate the selected principles into scene, depth, material, light, overlap, and visual-movement language for the image model; do not trace the SVG geometry.

Mode A may use the translated grammar while generating the whole poster. Mode B must first ask an image-generation model for a real PNG, WebP, or JPEG artistic base, then composite protected people, screenshots, Logos, and accurate copy. No guide below may create or substitute for that base.

## Grammar cards

### LG01 — Single hero

- First visual: the creator or a creator-plus-signature-action combination.
- Supporting layer: one short idea statement and no more than two proof blocks.
- Use when: the account's recognizable mechanism is more persuasive than a roster.
- Avoid: shrinking the creator to make room for generic strategy copy.

### LG02 — Left copy, right creator

- First visual: title and creator should balance, not compete.
- Supporting layer: one rationale, one play, or a compact evidence row.
- Use when: the proposal needs explanation but still depends on a strong person image.
- Avoid: treating the right side as an unrelated cutout parking area.

### LG03 — Unified ensemble

- First visual: a compact group with clear front, middle, and back layers.
- Supporting layer: one shared theme and only the most important evidence.
- Use when: all creators serve the same content mechanism.
- Avoid: equal-size lineup, disconnected gaps, hidden faces, duplicate subjects, and hard cutout boundaries.

### LG04 — Grouped by play

- First visual: each play contains its own people and evidence.
- Supporting layer: stable titles, color codes, or numbering connect the groups.
- Use when: creator differences are meaningful to the proposal.
- Avoid: one separate master collage that forces the reader to remap people to plays.

### LG05 — Three play columns

- First visual: one large title plus three equally legible play worlds.
- Supporting layer: each column owns a title, one-sentence mechanism, creators, and proof.
- Use when: the plays are genuinely parallel.
- Avoid: pretending unequal or sequential ideas are equal columns.

### LG06 — Dense matrix

- First visual: title and one organizing device such as numbering, a grid, or color coding.
- Supporting layer: strategy, creator mapping, proof, and cooperation information remain scan-friendly.
- Use when: removing information would weaken the proposal.
- Avoid: solving density by shrinking type; solve it through grouping, alignment, repetition, and scale contrast.

### LG07 — Central hero with satellites

- First visual: a central theme, action, or creator.
- Supporting layer: satellite groups show variations or extensions.
- Use when: several expressions clearly originate from one idea.
- Avoid: decorative orbit shapes without a real content relationship.

### LG08 — Sequential route

- First visual: the route or numbered phase logic.
- Supporting layer: people and evidence are attached to the phase where they act.
- Use when: the Brief contains a true sequence, relay, conversion path, or campaign phase.
- Avoid: inventing a route merely because arrows look dynamic.

## Surface-style rule

Colors, materials, containers, and motifs must point back to at least one of these sources:

- explicit Brief wording;
- audience emotion;
- creator or account evidence;
- brand rules;
- the content mechanism itself.

Do not infer `technology = blue neon glass UI`, `food = orange festival`, or any other category cliché without evidence. When evidence is weak, keep the surface system neutral and label the missing input.
